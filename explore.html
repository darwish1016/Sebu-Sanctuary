Water lilies on Lake Sebu

A highland escape in the south of the Philippines.

Lake Sebu is a mountain town in South Cotabato, on the island of Mindanao. Up here the air is cooler, the mornings are misty and the days are slow.

There are three lakes (Lake Sebu, Lake Seloton and Lake Lahit), waterfalls hidden in the forest, rice fields and farms, and the living culture of the Tboli people, whose ancestral home this is.

  • 8 min to Seven Falls
  • 8 min to the Lake Sebu market
  • 3 lakes Sebu, Seloton & Lahit
  • ~120 km from General Santos airport

Seven Falls & the zipline

Lake Sebu's best-known sight: a chain of seven waterfalls tumbling through the forest. The first falls are an easy walk, while the lower ones take a longer hike down to the valley floor. For the brave, a zipline flies you high above the falls with the whole valley below.

  • Go in the morning for softer light and fewer people.
  • Wear shoes with good grip for the trails.
  • Local crafts are sold near the entrance.

The lakes: Lake Sebu & Lake Seloton

Calm water, lotus flowers and floating fish farms. Take a canoe out at sunrise when the lake is still, join a lake cruise with a meal on board, and try tilapia fresh from the water, the local speciality.

  • Mornings are the calmest time to be on the water.
  • Order the tilapia grilled or fried, the way locals do.
Natural swimming pool

T'Daan Kini nature pool & falls

A quieter favourite: a natural pool of clear, cool water below forest waterfalls, perfect for a swim on a warm afternoon and a picnic on the rocks.

  • Bring water shoes, as the rocks can be slippery.
  • Ask your host about the best time to go.
Farm visit

Sebul Cacao Farm

Walk among the cacao trees, see how the pods are harvested and the seeds prepared, then taste hot chocolate made from the farm's own cacao. The farm is full of tropical flowers and fruit trees too.

Right from our door

Rice fields & mountain walks

Trails behind the sanctuary lead through farmland, rice fields and forest, with views across the valley. Early mornings bring mist, birdsong and butterflies; evenings bring golden light over the hills.

Lake Sebu public market

The town market is the place for fresh produce, lake fish, tropical fruit like marang, and everyday local life. It's also next to the bus depot, so it's where most journeys up the mountain begin and end.

Tboli culture

The Tboli people of Lake Sebu.

The Tboli are one of the indigenous peoples of southern Mindanao, and the highlands around Lake Sebu are their ancestral home. Their culture lives on in music, dance, dress and craft, and in a deep connection to the land and lakes.

T'nalak, the cloth of dreams

Tboli women are known as dream weavers. T'nalak is woven by hand from abaca fibre, with patterns that traditionally come to the weaver in her dreams, and a single piece can take months to make. The late Lang Dulay, named a National Living Treasure in 1998, taught many of today's weavers, and her family continues the work in Lake Sebu.

Music, dance & celebration

Gongs, the two-stringed hegelung lute and traditional dance accompany Tboli celebrations. Each year in late May, the Helobung Festival marks the founding of Lake Sebu with music, dance and colour.

Visiting with respect

  1. 01Ask before photographing people, and accept a "no" kindly.
  2. 02Buy directly from weavers and makers, so your money supports their families.
  3. 03Follow your local guide's lead at homes and cultural sites.
  4. 04T'nalak is treated with great respect. Please don't step on, sit on or cut it.

Shared with the community

We're developing this page and our cultural visits together with members of the local Tboli community, so that what we share is accurate, respectful and benefits the people whose culture it is.
